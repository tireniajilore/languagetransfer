# Pedagogy: splitting lessons & writing the teaching voice

Language Transfer's "thinking method" has the learner *assemble known pieces*,
not memorize and not cold-guess. Adapting it to a one-way TikTok feed means
keeping that feel while accepting you can't have real back-and-forth.

## Splitting one lesson into micro-lessons

A single LT lesson holds several postable ideas. Find the seams where one small,
self-contained "move" lives. Each becomes a standalone video.

Example — LT Spanish Lesson 2 split into three:
- **Vowels** — Spanish vowels never change (a-e-i-o-u = ah-eh-ee-oh-oo).
- **`es`** — one word = is / it is / he is / she is.
- **`no es`** — negate by putting `no` in front.

Each is ~30–60s, opens cold, and pays off one idea.

## The standalone rule

No "part two", no "as we saw last time". Re-teach the tiny bit you depend on.
LT itself recaps constantly and briefly — lean into that. A viewer who lands on
clip 3 with no context should still get a complete, satisfying lesson.

## Teach, THEN prompt (the most important rule)

Give the building block first; only then ask the viewer to use it, with examples
they're now equipped to answer. **Never prompt for a piece you haven't taught.**

Why it matters: LT's "thinking" is assembling known parts, not guessing an
untaught one. Quizzing something cold sets the viewer up to fail — and on a TikTok
open, a viewer who "fails" feels dumb and scrolls, the opposite of a hook.

- Wrong: show `natural`, say "Say this in Spanish, go" *before* teaching the
  vowels. (Quiz-first.)
- Right: teach the five fixed vowels → *then* "your turn: natural, metal, legal,
  ideal." Now they can get it.

The on-screen `SAY IT` + countdown is the "your turn" beat — it must land on
something already taught, so the pause is a win, not a trap.

## Payoff density: test earlier than feels natural

TikTok lessons should not stack several facts before the first win. If the
script has 4–5 explanation lines in a row before a prompt, it is probably too
front-loaded. Move a test earlier, or split the lesson.

Default cadence:
- Teach 1 small piece.
- Prompt a word or tiny sentence.
- Reveal with a short Spanish carrier.
- Teach the next piece.
- Prompt again.
- Only then combine into a longer sentence.

Examples from the Spanish sequence:
- Good: `es` → `urgente` → test `Es urgente` → `importante` → test
  `Es importante` → `realmente` → test `Realmente es urgente`.
- Good: `no es` → `importante` → test `No es importante` → `diferente` → test
  `No es diferente` → `normalmente` → test `Normalmente no es diferente`.
- Good: `posible` → test `Es posible` → `imposible` → test
  `No es imposible` → `mente` → test `Posiblemente es posible`.

For `mente` lessons, do not assume prior `mente` knowledge unless the clip is
explicitly a review. Teach or remind the mapping inside the clip before asking
for `realmente`, `normalmente`, `posiblemente`, or `probablemente`.

End combo/checkpoint lessons with one stretch prompt: a sentence the viewer has
not seen as a full sentence, but can assemble from known pieces. This creates the
"wait, I can say that?" payoff without cold-quizzing an untaught word.

## Hooks: promise/claim, not cold quiz

Line one is the scroll-stop. Make it a promise or a claim, never a cold
production quiz. Good hooks from this project:
- "Reading Spanish out loud is way easier than English. Here's the reason."
- "One tiny Spanish word builds a whole pile of sentences."
- "The easiest negative in Spanish. You'll have it in one move."
- "If you can say it in Spanish, you can already say the opposite." (ties to the
  brand line "you already speak Spanish")
- "This is why Spanish feels hackable."
- "This is the beginner Spanish sentence I wish people learned earlier."

Avoid hooks that sound like a lesson objective ("Today we will learn...") or
give away the whole rule too plainly. TikTok hooks can be a little more native:
curiosity, payoff, or a claim first; explanation second.

## Teaching voice (keep it LT, not app-narrator)

The failure mode is clean narration that an app voice could read. Add the live,
thinking-with-you texture, compressed for TikTok — pick a few per clip, don't
bloat:
- **Process affirmation** — name what they did right, not just the answer: "Good.
  You didn't reach for the English vowel." / "You didn't conjugate a thing."
- **Name what stayed still** — "You didn't learn a new ending. You just parked
  'no' in front." (Very LT.)
- **Anticipate the English interference** — "keep the S clean — not 'ez.'" / "If
  that came out NATCH-rull, that was English grabbing the wheel."
- **Use "we"** — "Let's see what changed", not "The rule is".
- **One delight beat** — "Same letter. Good luck." / "Spanish negatives are kind
  to you."
- **Engineer the pause** — a real silent beat before each reveal, plus "say it
  before I do." The gap is where the learner produces the answer; without it the
  "I did it myself" payoff (the thing people save/share for) never happens.

## Accuracy traps

- **Don't overgeneralize a scoped rule.** "Stress at the end" is true for `-al`
  cognates (animal, metal) but NOT general Spanish (most words stress the
  second-to-last). Scope it: "for these -al words…". A wrong mental model bites
  the learner later.
- **Watch audio-only ambiguity.** "Not natural." spoken can be heard as the
  negation; carry such contrasts visually or rephrase.
