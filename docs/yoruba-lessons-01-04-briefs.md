## 1. Lesson-Writing Protocol

Use this protocol before writing any full script.

### A. Select the main atom

Pick exactly one main atom from the panorama. If the lesson appears to teach two new thoughts, split it or make one a silent exposure.

### B. Check prerequisites

The learner can only use:

- atoms taught in earlier lessons,
- words introduced inside the lesson,
- patterns that match English unless explicitly contradicted.

If an answer requires a not-yet-taught atom, rewrite the prompt.

### C. Name the import

Each lesson should import something the learner already knows. It may be:

- a bodily skill, such as holding pitch,
- an English thought distinction, such as wanting a thing vs wanting to do an action,
- a familiar social scene, such as asking for water or greeting an elder.

Do not use loanwords just for comfort.

### D. Pick a narrow sentence frame

The first usable frame should be stable enough to survive swaps. The learner should feel, "I can keep this frame and change one piece."

### E. Define delayed temptations

Write down the true things we are refusing to explain. This protects the lesson from being eaten by the writer's knowledge.

### F. Draft the tension contour

Each lesson needs:

1. orientation,
2. first new thought,
3. controlled prompt,
4. masked repetition,
5. small friction,
6. recovery,
7. close with confidence, not a grammar dump.

### G. Script with audio-only language

Never say "letter," "mark," "accent mark," "spelled," "written," or anything that assumes a visible transcript. The teacher can say "listen," "my voice goes higher," "hold that sound," or "say it again slowly."

### H. Replay every learner answer

After every learner attempt, the teacher must immediately repeat the correct target answer before giving explanation, praise, correction, or the next prompt.

Required pattern:

```md
**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ omi. Middle voice at the front.
```

Do not write:

```md
**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Middle voice at the front.
```

This is not optional. The learner needs to hear the correct Yoruba again immediately after producing it.

### I. Add rater risks

Every draft script ends with specific native-review questions. The rater must know what we are uncertain about.

### J. Add teacher-voice planning

Before scripting, write these five items:

- **Teacher stance:** what the teacher believes the learner is learning about thinking, not just Yoruba.
- **Likely learner errors:** at least three wrong turns the learner may make.
- **Diagnostic corrections:** gentle corrections that name why the error happened.
- **Process affirmations:** places where feedback praises the mental move, not just the answer.
- **Functional aside:** one short aside that resets load, creates a hook, or raises language consciousness.

The teacher should sound warm, exact, curious, and live. If the script could be read by a generic app narrator without losing anything, it fails.

Use varied prompts:

- "How would you say...?"
- "What was...?"
- "What changed?"
- "What stayed still?"
- "Build it in two pieces."
- "Listen first."
- "Don't reach for memory; rebuild it."

See `docs/lt-spanish-teaching-style-review.md` for the full style analysis.

## 2. Micro-Arc Shape

Lessons 1-4 are one proof arc. The learner leaves the arc with:

- a body-level sense of Yoruba tone as held pitch,
- one productive sentence engine: `mo fẹ́ ...`,
- no article before nouns,
- no automatic English "to" before verbs,
- informal `o` contrasted with `mo`,
- high-tone `ó` contrasted with mid-tone `o`,
- the idea that Yoruba verbs do not change for person.

Out of scope for Lessons 1-4:

- negation,
- `Ṣé` questions,
- formal/respectful `ẹ`,
- plural `a/ẹ/wọn`,
- `láti`,
- object pronouns,
- tense/aspect,
- spelling/diacritics.

## 3. Lesson 1 Brief

### Main atom

Tone as held pitch plus the first productive frame: `Mo fẹ́ ...`.

### Learner thought

"Yoruba words have pitch built into them, but I do not need to master the whole tone system before I can build a sentence. I can say `I want ...` and change the final piece."

### Prerequisites

None.

### New material

- `mo`: I
- `fẹ́`: want
- `omi`: water
- `oúnjẹ`: food
- `jẹun`: eat / eat food
- `sùn`: sleep

### Recycled inside the lesson

- no articles: `Mo fẹ́ omi`, not "a water"
- SVO order: subject, verb, object
- verb-after-verb: `Mo fẹ́ jẹun`, not a separate English "to"
- tone levels by voice: mid, high, low

### Delayed temptations

- `o` and `ó`
- negation
- `Ṣé`
- `láti`
- `jẹ` vs `jẹun` analysis
- dotted vowels and tone marks

### Tension contour

1. Explain participation: pause, think, answer aloud, do not memorize.
2. Body import: hum/hold pitch.
3. Build `mo`.
4. Build `fẹ́`.
5. Combine `Mo fẹ́`.
6. Add nouns: water, food.
7. Add verbs: eat, sleep.
8. Contrast "want a thing" and "want to do something."
9. Recovery drill with only four possible endings.
10. Close with one sentence the learner can try today.

### Rater risks

- Is `Mo fẹ́ jẹun` the right beginner form for "I want to eat"?
- Is `oúnjẹ` the right word for food in this beginner context?
- Are the pitch descriptions natural enough for a native speaker to perform?
- Is the heritage-family line emotionally right or too much?

## 4. Lesson 2 Brief

### Main atom

Same frame, more control. The learner keeps `Mo fẹ́ ...` while swapping between things and actions.

### Learner thought

"I do not need a new grammar explanation every time. I can reuse the same frame and let new vocabulary ride inside it."

### Prerequisites

- SND-01: tone orientation
- SND-02: three tone levels by voice
- SENT-01: no articles
- SENT-02: basic SVO order
- SENT-05: verb-after-verb feel
- PRON-01: `mo`
- ACT-02: `fẹ́ + noun`
- ACT-03: `fẹ́ + verb`

### New material

Keep vocabulary narrow. Candidate set for native review:

- `lọ`: go
- `wá`: come
- `rà`: buy
- `mu`: drink
- one practical noun such as `owó` money or `búrẹ́dì` bread

### Recycled atoms

- `Mo fẹ́ omi`
- `Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ`
- `Mo fẹ́ jẹun`
- `Mo fẹ́ sùn`
- no English "to"
- tones as held pitch, not sliding

### Delayed temptations

- `sí` after motion verbs
- object pronouns
- `láti`
- future `máa`
- questions

### Tension contour

1. Start with a quick rebuild of Lesson 1 from prompts.
2. Add one new action.
3. Show that `fẹ́` still does not change.
4. Add one noun.
5. Alternate noun endings and verb endings.
6. Small friction: English says "to go," Yoruba keeps the verb next to `fẹ́`.
7. Close by previewing that next lesson changes the person, not the verb.

### Script notes

Lesson 2 should feel lighter than Lesson 1. It is a confidence and recycling lesson, not a grammar-expansion lesson.

## 5. Lesson 3 Brief

### Main atom

`o` as informal singular "you."

### Learner thought

"To change who wants something, I change the small word at the front. The verb stays relaxed."

### Prerequisites

- Lesson 1 frame
- Lesson 2 recycling
- tone as held pitch

### New material

- `o`: you, speaking to one person informally

### Recycled atoms

- `fẹ́` unchanged
- noun endings and verb endings
- SVO
- no English "to"
- no articles

### Delayed temptations

- `ó`
- `ẹ` respectful/plural you
- `Ṣé o fẹ́ ...?`
- object pronoun `ẹ`
- commands

### Tension contour

1. Rebuild `Mo fẹ́ ...`.
2. Ask what changes in English: I want vs you want.
3. Introduce `o`.
4. Swap `Mo fẹ́ omi` / `O fẹ́ omi`.
5. Swap with actions: `Mo fẹ́ sùn` / `O fẹ́ sùn`.
6. Make the learner notice `fẹ́` did not change.
7. Recovery drill with "I" and "you."
8. Close by warning that another pronoun sounds almost the same but sits higher in the voice.

### Rater risks

- Whether the informal `o` should be introduced before any respect framing for a heritage audience.
- How to avoid learners using informal `o` with elders before Lesson 6 introduces `ẹ`.

## 6. Lesson 4 Brief

### Main atom

`ó` as he/she/it, contrasted with `o`.

### Learner thought

"The same mouth shape can be a different Yoruba word when the voice sits at a different pitch."

### Prerequisites

- `mo`
- `o`
- `fẹ́`
- tone as held pitch

### New material

- `ó`: he/she/it

### Recycled atoms

- `o` informal you
- `mo`
- `fẹ́` unchanged across persons
- tone levels
- noun/action endings

### Delayed temptations

- object `ó` / copied vowel
- `ó` dropping before `kò`
- `wọn`
- gendered English explanations beyond "he/she/it"
- negation

### Tension contour

1. Rebuild `Mo fẹ́ ...` and `O fẹ́ ...`.
2. Introduce `ó` by voice, not by marks.
3. Contrast `o` and `ó`.
4. Drill `O fẹ́ omi` vs `Ó fẹ́ omi`.
5. Show English changes want/wants but Yoruba `fẹ́` stays the same.
6. Small friction: "she wants" and "he wants" are the same here.
7. Recovery drill mixing `mo`, `o`, `ó`.
8. Close by naming the big win: the learner can change the person without conjugating the verb.

### Rater risks

- Whether the `o` / `ó` contrast is safe and clear enough for a non-literate audio learner.
- Whether `ó` should be introduced with he/she/it or only he/she at first.

## 7. Result of This Trial

The protocol works well enough to draft from, but it immediately changes Lesson 1:

- v3 tried to teach `mo`, `o`, `ó`, negation, tone, no articles, no conjugation, and no English "to" in one lesson.
- v4 should teach only the first frame and tone orientation.
- Lessons 3 and 4 now carry the pronoun/tone contrast.
- Negation moves out of the micro-arc entirely.

That is exactly the kind of correction TMG's "one thought at a time" rule is supposed to force.
