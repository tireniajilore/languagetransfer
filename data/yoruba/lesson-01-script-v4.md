# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 1, Draft v4

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 12-15 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: tone as held pitch plus the first productive frame, `Mo fẹ́ ...`.

What changed from v3:

- Removed `o` and `ó`; they now belong to Lessons 3 and 4.
- Removed negation; it now belongs later in the course.
- Kept one sentence engine and drilled it harder.
- Switched "I want to eat" candidate to `Mo fẹ́ jẹun`, pending native review.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This is an audio-only course. The script is a performance document. The listener hears only your voice. They will not see spelling, tone marks, or Yoruba text.

Please read teacher lines slowly and warmly. Where the script has `[pause]`, hold about three seconds so the learner can try the answer before hearing it.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `Mo fẹ́ jẹun` the best beginner sentence for "I want to eat"?
2. Is `oúnjẹ` the right beginner word for "food" in this lesson?
3. Are the pitch descriptions accurate enough for a native speaker to perform?
4. Does anything sound too formal, too textbook-like, or unnatural for everyday Yoruba?
5. Would you give this to a younger cousin or diaspora learner?

## Script

### Track 1 — How to Use This Course

**Teacher:** Welcome. Before Yoruba, one important thing about how to use this course.

**Teacher:** I will ask you to build things. When I ask, pause the audio. Take your time. Say your answer out loud. Then continue and hear the answer.

**Teacher:** These are not tests. You are not trying to be fast. You are learning to think slowly and speak from that thinking.

**Teacher:** Also, don't try to memorize. Memorizing will pull you out of the work. Just understand each little move as we make it.

**Teacher:** Good. Let's begin.

---

### Track 2 — The Voice Skill

**Teacher:** Yoruba uses something your voice can already do.

**Teacher:** Hum one note, any note, and hold it.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** Good. Now hum a higher note, and hold that.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** Good. Now hum a lower note, and hold that.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** That's the skill. Holding your voice steady at different heights.

**Teacher:** In English, pitch usually shows feeling. In Yoruba, pitch can be part of the word itself. So don't slide around looking for an English melody. Just listen, then copy where my voice sits.

---

### Track 3 — I

**Teacher:** The Yoruba word for "I" is **mo**. Mo.

**Teacher:** Say it.

**Student:** Mo.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo. Small word. Middle of the voice. Say it once more.

**Student:** Mo.

---

### Track 4 — Want

**Teacher:** The word for "want" is **fẹ́**. Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** My voice sits higher there. Fẹ́. Try it.

**Student:** Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo means I. Fẹ́ means want.

**Teacher:** How would you say "I want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́. I want.

**Teacher:** Say it again, and hear the little lift from mo to fẹ́.

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

---

### Track 5 — Wanting a Thing

**Teacher:** The word for water is **omi**. Omi.

**Teacher:** Two little beats, both in the middle of the voice. Omi. Say it.

**Student:** Omi.

**Teacher:** Good. If mo fẹ́ is "I want," how would you say "I want water"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Notice there was no word for "a." Not "I want a water." Just: I want water. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Again. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

---

### Track 6 — Another Thing

**Teacher:** The word for food is **oúnjẹ**. Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Listen first. Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. So how do you say "I want food"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Let's swap only the last piece. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

---

### Track 7 — Wanting to Do Something

**Teacher:** Sometimes you want a thing. Water. Food.

**Teacher:** Sometimes you want to do something.

**Teacher:** In English we say "I want to eat." Yoruba does not need that English "to" here. The next action can come straight after want.

**Teacher:** The word for eat is **jẹun**. Jẹun.

**Teacher:** Say it.

**Student:** Jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. So if mo fẹ́ is "I want," and jẹun is "eat," how would you say "I want to eat"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** We thought "I want to eat" in English, but we did not carry the English "to" into Yoruba. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 8 — One More Action

**Teacher:** The word for sleep is **sùn**. Sùn.

**Teacher:** My voice sits low there. Sùn. Try it.

**Student:** Sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Now say "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Listen to the shape: mo, middle. Fẹ́, higher. Sùn, lower. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

---

### Track 9 — The Frame

**Teacher:** Now we have one frame.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ ... I want ...

**Teacher:** After that, we can put a thing or an action.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Again, but a little quicker. "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 10 — Close

**Teacher:** Good. Stop there.

**Teacher:** You did not memorize four phrases. You built four sentences from one frame.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi. I want water.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ. I want food.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun. I want to eat.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ sùn. I want to sleep.

**Teacher:** The frame stayed the same. The ending changed.

**Teacher:** Next lesson, we will keep this same frame and make it feel more automatic. No new grammar mountain. Just more control.

**Teacher:** For now, pick one sentence and say it out loud once today. Slowly. Let your voice sit where it needs to sit.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mo` | I | high | subject pronoun |
| `fẹ́` | want | high | needs tone-certified native reading |
| `omi` | water | high | source-stable |
| `oúnjẹ` | food | medium | verify beginner naturalness |
| `jẹun` | eat | medium | verify vs `jẹ` in this frame |
| `sùn` | sleep | high | low tone needs performance check |

Structures introduced:

- tone as held pitch,
- `Mo fẹ́ ...`,
- no indefinite article,
- same frame for noun endings and verb endings,
- no English infinitive "to" after `fẹ́`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `o`,
- `ó`,
- negation,
- `Ṣé`,
- `láti`,
- spelling/diacritics,
- tense/aspect.
