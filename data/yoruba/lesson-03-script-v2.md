# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 3, Draft v2

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 13-16 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: `o` as informal singular "you."

Teacher stance: the learner is learning to change the front of the sentence without disturbing the verb or the ending.

Likely learner errors:

- changing `fẹ́` because English changes "want/wants" later,
- using informal `o` too broadly without respecting register,
- confusing the front pronoun with the object "you,"
- trying to turn statements into questions too early.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson introduces informal singular `o` but does not yet introduce respectful/plural `ẹ`. Please check whether that is pedagogically acceptable for a heritage learner if the teacher clearly says this is familiar/informal and that a respectful form is coming later.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `o` the right form to introduce here?
2. Are the sample sentences natural?
3. Does the respect warning feel sufficient without teaching `ẹ` yet?
4. Is it okay that the learner can say "you want water" before they can ask "do you want water?"
5. Does the teacher voice teach the process clearly?

---

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild the Machine

**Teacher:** Welcome back. First, prove to yourself that the frame is still there.

**Teacher:** What was "I want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ. You rebuilt, you didn't rummage around for a memorized phrase. That is the skill.

---

### Track 2 — The Front Does the Work

**Teacher:** Today we change the front of the sentence.

**Teacher:** In English, listen:

**Teacher:** I want.

**Teacher:** You want.

**Teacher:** Nothing happened to "want." Only "I" became "you."

**Teacher:** Good news: Yoruba is going to let us do something very similar here.

---

### Track 3 — Familiar You

**Teacher:** The Yoruba word for "you," talking to one person in a familiar way, is **o**. O.

**Teacher:** Listen: o.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** O.

**Teacher:** Good. O. Small word, middle of the voice.

**Teacher:** Now, careful. This is a familiar "you." Useful, but not for everyone in the family. Yoruba cares about respect, and we will give you that respectful form soon.

**Teacher:** For today, imagine a sibling, a cousin, a friend. One person, familiar: o.

---

### Track 4 — You Want

**Teacher:** Build this in two pieces.

**Teacher:** First, what is "you"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** O.

**Teacher:** Good. O. Now add want. "You want."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** What stayed still from `Mo fẹ́`?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Exactly. Fẹ́. That's the win. The person changed and the verb didn't flinch.

---

### Track 5 — I or You

**Teacher:** Now we swap only the front.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ omi. Same ending. Same verb. Different front.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Possible learner mistake:** O fẹ́ to jẹun.

**Teacher:** If you catch English trying to add "to" later, the fix is the same as before. Front, frame, ending. O fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 6 — Actions

**Teacher:** Let's use actions now.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ sùn. You changed `mo` to `o`; everything else survived.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "You want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Very good. O fẹ́ jẹun. That is the frame becoming flexible.

---

### Track 7 — Not a Question Yet

**Teacher:** A little aside, because English may be getting impatient.

**Teacher:** "You want water" is a statement. We are not asking "Do you want water?" yet.

**Teacher:** That question is coming. But if we bring it in now, it will steal attention from today's job.

**Teacher:** Today's job is simpler and deeper: change the person at the front.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** That's enough for one clean thought.

---

### Track 8 — Front and Ending

**Teacher:** Think of two places.

**Teacher:** The front tells us who.

**Teacher:** The ending tells us what is wanted.

**Teacher:** Build "you want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ owó. Now "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "You want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ wá. Your mind is learning where the moving parts are.

---

**Teacher:** Final round. I will mix I and familiar you.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

---

### Track 10 — Close

**Teacher:** Today we added one small word and opened a lot of space.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́. I want.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́. You want.

**Teacher:** Fẹ́ stayed the same.

**Teacher:** Next lesson we meet a word that sounds dangerously close to `o`, but sits higher in the voice. This is where tone stops being an idea and becomes something you can feel.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `o` | you, singular informal/familiar | medium | native review needed for audience/register framing |

Structures introduced:

- changing the front pronoun changes the person,
- `fẹ́` stays unchanged with `mo` and `o`,
- familiar/informal warning without teaching respectful `ẹ` yet.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `ó`,
- respectful/plural `ẹ`,
- `Ṣé`,
- object pronoun `ẹ`,
- commands,
- negation.
