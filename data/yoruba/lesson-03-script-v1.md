# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 3, Draft v1

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 12-15 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: `o` as informal singular "you."

Purpose: show that the front pronoun changes while `fẹ́` stays the same.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson introduces informal singular `o` but does not yet introduce respectful/plural `ẹ`. Please check whether that is pedagogically acceptable for a heritage learner if the teacher clearly says this is familiar/informal and that a respectful form is coming later.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `o` the right form to introduce here?
2. Are the sample sentences natural?
3. Does the respect warning feel sufficient without teaching `ẹ` yet?
4. Is it okay that the learner can say "you want water" before they can ask "do you want water?"

---

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild the Frame

**Teacher:** Welcome back. Let's rebuild the frame.

**Teacher:** "I want."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Same frame. Different endings.

---

### Track 2 — Changing the Person

**Teacher:** Today we change the person.

**Teacher:** In English, listen to this:

**Teacher:** I want.

**Teacher:** You want.

**Teacher:** The word "want" did not change. Only the person changed.

**Teacher:** Yoruba is going to be kind to us here.

---

### Track 3 — You

**Teacher:** The Yoruba word for "you," talking to one person in a familiar way, is **o**. O.

**Teacher:** Say it.

**Student:** O.

**Teacher:** Good. O.

**Teacher:** This is for one person, familiar or informal. Yoruba has a different form for respect, like when speaking to an elder. We are not using that one yet.

**Teacher:** For now, just one familiar person: o.

---

### Track 4 — You Want

**Teacher:** If mo fẹ́ means "I want," and o means "you," how would you say "you want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Notice what did not change: fẹ́. The want stayed the same.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́. I want.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́. You want.

**Teacher:** Say both.

**Student:** Mo fẹ́. O fẹ́.

---

### Track 5 — First Swaps

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. The ending stayed the same. Only the front changed.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

---

### Track 6 — Actions

**Teacher:** Now actions.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "You want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Fẹ́ did not move. It did not change its shape. The front changed.

---

### Track 7 — The Front and the Ending

**Teacher:** Think of the sentence as two places.

**Teacher:** The front tells us who.

**Teacher:** The ending tells us what is wanted.

**Teacher:** Mo ... I.

**Teacher:** O ... you.

**Teacher:** Omi ... water.

**Teacher:** Sùn ... sleep.

**Teacher:** Now build this: "you want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "You want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

---

### Track 8 — Final Round

**Teacher:** Final round. I will mix I and you.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good.

---

### Track 9 — Close

**Teacher:** Today you changed the person at the front.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́. I want.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́. You want.

**Teacher:** Fẹ́ stayed the same.

**Teacher:** Next lesson, we meet a tiny word that sounds almost the same as o, but your voice sits higher. That difference matters.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `o` | you, singular informal/familiar | medium | native review needed for audience/register framing |

Structures introduced:

- changing the front pronoun changes the person,
- `fẹ́` stays unchanged with `mo` and `o`,
- familiar/informal warning without teaching respectful `ẹ` yet.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `ó`,
- respectful/plural `ẹ`,
- `Ṣé`,
- object pronoun `ẹ`,
- commands,
- negation.
