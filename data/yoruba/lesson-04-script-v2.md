# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 4, Draft v2

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 14-17 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: `ó` as he/she/it, contrasted with `o`.

Teacher stance: the learner is learning that tone is not decoration; it can be the difference between two grammatical words.

Likely learner errors:

- flattening `ó` into `o`,
- over-pronouncing the contrast as a melody instead of a held pitch,
- expecting separate words for he and she,
- changing `fẹ́` to match English "wants."

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson depends on the performer clearly contrasting `o` and `ó` by voice. Please check whether this is safe as an audio-only lesson.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `ó` correctly used for he/she/it in these beginner sentences?
2. Is the `o` / `ó` contrast clear and natural enough?
3. Should `ó` be introduced as he/she only first, or he/she/it?
4. Are any examples unnatural without more context?
5. Does the teacher voice support the tone contrast well?

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild I and You

**Teacher:** Welcome back. Let's wake up the frame.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi. "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ sùn. Mo at the front for I. O at the front for familiar you.

**Teacher:** Today we keep that same frame, but your ear has to do slightly finer work.

---

### Track 2 — Listen Before Meaning

**Teacher:** Listen first. Don't translate yet.

**Teacher:** O.

**Teacher:** Ó.

**Teacher:** Again.

**Teacher:** O.

**Teacher:** Ó.

**Teacher:** The mouth shape is almost the same. The place in the voice is not.

**Teacher:** O sits in the middle. Ó sits higher.

**Teacher:** Try them slowly.

**Student:** O. Ó.

**Teacher:** Good. O. Ó. If you slid upward like a little question, try again more simply. Land in the middle: o. Land higher: ó.

---

### Track 3 — The New Front

**Teacher:** You already know **o** as familiar "you."

**Teacher:** The higher one, **ó**, means he, she, or it.

**Teacher:** Listen again with meaning now.

**Teacher:** O. You.

**Teacher:** Ó. He, she, or it.

**Teacher:** Say them.

**Student:** O. Ó.

**Teacher:** Good. O. Ó. This is why we did not want to rush tone. Here it earns its keep.

---

### Track 4 — He or She Wants

**Teacher:** Build this carefully.

**Teacher:** If `o fẹ́` is "you want," how would you say "he wants"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** What changed?

*[pause]*

**Student:** The front.

**Teacher:** Good. The front. What stayed still?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Exactly. Fẹ́. English says he wants. Yoruba here keeps `fẹ́` still. The verb doesn't flinch.

**Teacher:** And "she wants"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́. Same Yoruba. Context will tell us whether we mean he or she.

---

### Track 5 — You or He

**Teacher:** Now we make the contrast work.

**Teacher:** Listen to the English prompt. Decide whether the front is `o` or `ó`.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ omi. Middle voice at the front.

**Teacher:** "He wants water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ omi. Higher at the front.

**Possible learner mistake:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** If you said `o fẹ́ omi` for "he wants water," the structure was fine. Only the pitch picked the wrong person. That is a good kind of mistake: small, audible, fixable. Ó fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "She wants food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

---

### Track 6 — Three Fronts

**Teacher:** Now we have three fronts.

**Teacher:** Mo. I.

**Teacher:** O. You.

**Teacher:** Ó. He or she.

**Teacher:** Say them slowly.

**Student:** Mo. O. Ó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo. O. Ó. Not a chant, not a song. Just three small words with three jobs.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "You want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "She wants money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ owó. You changed the person and the rest of the sentence stayed calm.

---

### Track 7 — English Wants to Change the Verb

**Teacher:** English is going to make one more suggestion now.

**Teacher:** I want.

**Teacher:** You want.

**Teacher:** He wants.

**Teacher:** English changes want to wants.

**Teacher:** Yoruba does not need that here.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Same `fẹ́` every time.

**Teacher:** That is a relief. Take the relief where the language gives it to you.

---

### Track 8 — Actions

**Teacher:** Now with actions.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "She wants to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ sùn. Hear that high front and low ending: ó ... sùn.

**Teacher:** "He wants to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

---

### Track 9 — It

**Teacher:** Ó can also mean it.

**Teacher:** We do not need much of that yet, and I don't want it stealing the lesson. But you should hear the possibility.

**Teacher:** "It wants water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ omi. Same Yoruba sentence as "he wants water" or "she wants water." Context does the choosing.

**Teacher:** We will leave `it` there for now. Touched, not unpacked.

---

### Track 10 — Final Round

**Teacher:** Final round. This is ear work and structure work together.

**Teacher:** "You want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "She wants to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ jẹun. Tiny pitch difference, big meaning difference.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "He wants food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "You want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "She wants money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Very good. Ó fẹ́ owó. You held the frame, changed the front, and listened for pitch. That's real work.

---

### Track 11 — Close

**Teacher:** This first little arc has given you a working engine.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́. I want.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́. You want.

**Teacher:** Ó fẹ́. He wants. She wants. It wants.

**Teacher:** Same verb. Different front.

**Teacher:** And today the difference between `o` and `ó` was not decoration. It was grammar. Your voice carried meaning.

**Teacher:** That is a good place to stop.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ó` | he/she/it | medium | native performance must distinguish from `o` |

Structures introduced:

- pitch can distinguish `o` and `ó`,
- `ó` covers he/she/it,
- `fẹ́` remains unchanged across `mo`, `o`, and `ó`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- object `ó` / third-person copied vowel,
- `ó` dropping before `kò`,
- `wọn`,
- negation,
- questions,
- tense/aspect.
