# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 1, Draft v5

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 14-17 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: tone as held pitch plus the first productive frame, `Mo fẹ́ ...`.

Teacher stance: the learner is not memorizing Yoruba phrases. They are learning to hold a small frame steady while English tries to interfere.

Likely learner errors:

- sliding the voice instead of holding pitch,
- adding English "to" before a second verb,
- trying to remember the whole sentence instead of rebuilding the frame,
- flattening `fẹ́` after hearing several mid/low words.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This is an audio-only course. The script is a performance document. The listener hears only your voice. They will not see spelling, tone marks, or Yoruba text.

Please read teacher lines slowly and warmly. Where the script has `[pause]`, hold about three seconds so the learner can try the answer before hearing it.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `Mo fẹ́ jẹun` the best beginner sentence for "I want to eat"?
2. Is `oúnjẹ` the right beginner word for "food" in this lesson?
3. Are the pitch descriptions accurate enough for a native speaker to perform?
4. Does anything sound too formal, too textbook-like, or unnatural for everyday Yoruba?
5. Does the teacher voice feel like a real patient teacher, not a narrator?

## Script

### Track 1 — How to Work

**Teacher:** Welcome. Before we touch Yoruba, I want to set the rules of the game.

**Teacher:** I will ask you to build something. When I ask, pause the audio. Don't rush. Say your answer out loud. Then come back and listen.

**Teacher:** If you answer slowly, that is not a problem. That is the method working. Fast guessing feels nice, but slow thinking builds the language.

**Teacher:** And don't try to memorize. If you catch yourself thinking, "Do I remember this phrase?", gently move away from that. We are going to build the phrase again from smaller pieces.

**Teacher:** Good. Yoruba now.

---

### Track 2 — The Voice Skill

**Teacher:** Yoruba is going to ask your voice to do something it already knows how to do.

**Teacher:** Hum one note, any note, and hold it.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** Good. Now hum a higher note and hold it. Don't slide up to it like a question. Just land there and stay there.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** Good. Now a lower note. Hold that one too.

*[pause]*

**Teacher:** That's the useful skill. Not singing. Not performing. Just letting your voice sit somewhere.

**Teacher:** English uses pitch mostly for attitude: surprise, doubt, a question, maybe sarcasm if you're feeling dramatic. Yoruba can use pitch as part of the word itself.

**Teacher:** So today, don't try to understand the whole tone system. That would be too much. Just copy where the voice sits.

---

### Track 3 — I

**Teacher:** The Yoruba word for "I" is **mo**. Mo.

**Teacher:** Listen once: mo.

**Teacher:** Now say it.

**Student:** Mo.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo. Small word, middle of the voice. Don't lean on it. It just opens the door for the rest of the sentence.

**Teacher:** Again: mo.

**Student:** Mo.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo.

---

### Track 4 — Want

**Teacher:** The word for "want" is **fẹ́**. Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Listen before you say it. My voice is sitting higher there: fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Fẹ́. If it came out a little flat, don't wrestle with it. Just listen again: mo ... fẹ́. Middle, then higher.

**Teacher:** Mo means I. Fẹ́ means want.

**Teacher:** Build it in two pieces. How would you say "I want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good because you did not search for a whole phrase. You put the two pieces next to each other.

**Teacher:** Say it once more and let the second piece sit higher.

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́.

---

### Track 5 — Water

**Teacher:** The word for water is **omi**. Omi.

**Teacher:** Two little beats, both comfortable in the middle of the voice. Omi.

**Teacher:** Say it.

**Student:** Omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Omi. Now don't remember a phrase. Build it.

**Teacher:** First the frame: I want.

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́. Now add water. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi. You kept the frame still and only changed the ending. That's the habit we want.

**Teacher:** Notice also there was no word for "a." English might say "a water" in some situations, or "some water." Yoruba didn't ask us for that here. Just the thing: omi.

**Teacher:** Again. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

---

### Track 6 — Food

**Teacher:** The word for food is **oúnjẹ**. Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Listen first. Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Try it slowly.

**Student:** Oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Oúnjẹ. If your mouth felt busier there, that's fine. We are not stopping to study every sound. We are just using the word in the frame.

**Teacher:** How would you say "I want food"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** What stayed still?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Exactly. Mo fẹ́. The frame stayed still.

**Teacher:** Now swap only the last piece. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ. That is not memorizing. That is control.

---

### Track 7 — Eat

**Teacher:** Sometimes you want a thing. Water. Food.

**Teacher:** Sometimes you want to do something. English puts a little "to" there: I want to eat.

**Teacher:** English is going to try to donate that "to" to Yoruba. Don't accept the gift. Yoruba didn't ask for it here.

**Teacher:** The word for eat is **jẹun**. Jẹun.

**Teacher:** Say it.

**Student:** Jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Jẹun. So take it in two pieces. First, "I want."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Now add eat. "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Possible learner mistake:** Mo fẹ́ to jẹun.

**Teacher:** If you felt that English "to" trying to sneak in, excellent, notice it. That's exactly where English interferes. Go back to the Yoruba frame: mo fẹ́, then the action. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Again. "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 8 — Sleep

**Teacher:** One more action. Listen before you say this one.

**Teacher:** **Sùn**. Sùn.

**Teacher:** Let your voice sit low and stay there. Sùn.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Sùn. Now build "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Listen to the little path your voice just walked: mo, middle. Fẹ́, higher. Sùn, lower. Three small pieces, and each one has its own place.

**Teacher:** We are not making a chart of tones. We are letting your ear begin to trust itself.

---

### Track 9 — Mixed Building

**Teacher:** Now I will mix things and actions. Your job is always the same: find the frame, then add the ending.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi. The frame held.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun. No English "to."

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Very good. Mo fẹ́ sùn. Let's do a quicker round, but not rushed. Quick thinking is still thinking.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 10 — Close

**Teacher:** Good. Let's stop before we get greedy.

**Teacher:** You have one engine now:

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ ...

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi. I want water.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ. I want food.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun. I want to eat.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ sùn. I want to sleep.

**Teacher:** The win is not four phrases. The win is that the frame stayed still while you changed the ending.

**Teacher:** Next time, we will keep this frame and get more control. No new mountain. Just stronger footing.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mo` | I | high | subject pronoun |
| `fẹ́` | want | high | needs tone-certified native reading |
| `omi` | water | high | source-stable |
| `oúnjẹ` | food | medium | verify beginner naturalness |
| `jẹun` | eat | medium | verify vs `jẹ` in this frame |
| `sùn` | sleep | high | low tone needs performance check |

Structures introduced:

- tone as held pitch,
- `Mo fẹ́ ...`,
- no indefinite article,
- same frame for noun endings and verb endings,
- no English infinitive "to" after `fẹ́`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `o`,
- `ó`,
- negation,
- `Ṣé`,
- `láti`,
- spelling/diacritics,
- tense/aspect.
