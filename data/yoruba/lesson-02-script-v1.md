# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 2, Draft v1

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 12-15 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: same `Mo fẹ́ ...` frame, more control.

Purpose: make Lesson 1 feel usable before changing person in Lesson 3.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson deliberately adds very little grammar. It should feel like control, not expansion.

Please check:

1. Are `lọ`, `wá`, and `owó` appropriate beginner additions?
2. Are the tones marked well enough for a native speaker to perform?
3. Does `Mo fẹ́ wá` sound natural enough as a beginner drill sentence, or should it be replaced?
4. Is `Mo fẹ́ owó` acceptable as a simple drill sentence, or too blunt/odd?
5. Does the repetition feel useful or boring?

---

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild

**Teacher:** Welcome back. Don't try to remember Lesson 1. Rebuild it.

**Teacher:** The word for "I" was **mo**. Say it.

**Student:** Mo.

**Teacher:** Good. The word for "want" was **fẹ́**. Say it.

**Student:** Fẹ́.

**Teacher:** So how do you say "I want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** The word for water was **omi**. So: "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. And the word for sleep was **sùn**. "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Yes. Notice we rebuilt that. We did not dig it out of memory like a phrase. We built it again.

---

### Track 2 — The Frame

**Teacher:** This is the important thing from Lesson 1:

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ ...

**Teacher:** The frame stays. The ending changes.

**Teacher:** Say just the frame.

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. A thing: food. An action: eat. The same frame handled both.

---

### Track 3 — Go

**Teacher:** The word for go is **lọ**. Lọ.

**Teacher:** My voice sits low there. Lọ. Try it.

**Student:** Lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. So how would you say "I want to go"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** English had "to go." Yoruba here just lets the action follow want. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Again. "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

---

### Track 4 — Come

**Teacher:** The word for come is **wá**. Wá.

**Teacher:** My voice sits higher there. Wá. Try it.

**Student:** Wá.

**Teacher:** Good. So: "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Now listen to the endings.

**Teacher:** Lọ. Go. Low.

**Teacher:** Wá. Come. Higher.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

---

### Track 5 — One More Thing

**Teacher:** The word for money is **owó**. Owó.

**Teacher:** Listen first. Owó.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Owó.

**Teacher:** Good. So how do you say "I want money"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Again, no word for "some" or "a" here. Just the thing itself. Mo fẹ́ owó.

---

### Track 6 — Noun or Action

**Teacher:** Now I will mix things and actions. Take your time.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 7 — Small Friction

**Teacher:** Let's make the English a little louder for a moment.

**Teacher:** I want water.

**Teacher:** I want food.

**Teacher:** I want money.

**Teacher:** Those are things.

**Teacher:** I want to eat.

**Teacher:** I want to sleep.

**Teacher:** I want to go.

**Teacher:** I want to come.

**Teacher:** Those are actions.

**Teacher:** English changes shape. Sometimes there is a "to." Yoruba keeps the frame steady.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Same frame. Different ending.

---

### Track 8 — Final Round

**Teacher:** Final round. Build, don't remember.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 9 — Close

**Teacher:** Good. You now have one frame with several endings.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi. I want water.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ lọ. I want to go.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ owó. I want money.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ wá. I want to come.

**Teacher:** Next lesson, we will change the person. Not the verb. Just the little word at the front.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lọ` | go | medium | verify tone/performance |
| `wá` | come | medium | verify beginner naturalness in `Mo fẹ́ wá` |
| `owó` | money | medium | verify naturalness in `Mo fẹ́ owó` |

Structures introduced:

- no new grammar atom beyond stronger control of `Mo fẹ́ ...`,
- mixed noun endings and verb endings,
- continued delay of English infinitive `to`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `o`,
- `ó`,
- `sí`,
- object pronouns,
- negation,
- questions,
- future `máa`.
