# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 4, Draft v1

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 12-15 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: `ó` as he/she/it, contrasted with `o`.

Purpose: make the learner feel that pitch can distinguish words while keeping the `fẹ́` frame stable.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson depends on the performer clearly contrasting `o` and `ó` by voice. Please check whether this is safe as an audio-only lesson.

Critical review questions:

1. Is `ó` correctly used for he/she/it in these beginner sentences?
2. Is the `o` / `ó` contrast clear and natural enough?
3. Should `ó` be introduced as he/she only first, or he/she/it?
4. Are any examples unnatural without more context?

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild I and You

**Teacher:** Welcome back. Rebuild what you know.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo at the front for I. O at the front for you.

---

### Track 2 — A New Tiny Word

**Teacher:** Now listen carefully.

**Teacher:** O.

**Teacher:** Ó.

**Teacher:** My mouth is doing almost the same thing. But my voice is sitting in a different place.

**Teacher:** O is you, one familiar person.

**Teacher:** Ó is he, she, or it.

**Teacher:** Listen again.

**Teacher:** O.

**Teacher:** Ó.

**Teacher:** Try them.

**Student:** O. Ó.

**Teacher:** Good. Don't rush them. Let the first one sit in the middle of your voice. Let the second one sit higher.

---

### Track 3 — He or She Wants

**Teacher:** If o fẹ́ is "you want," how would you say "he wants"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Yes. Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** The front changed. Fẹ́ stayed the same.

**Teacher:** And "she wants" is the same here.

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Yoruba does not need to choose he or she in this word. Ó can cover both.

---

### Track 4 — You or He

**Teacher:** Now feel the difference.

**Teacher:** "You want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "He wants water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Same ending. Different front.

**Teacher:** "You want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "She wants food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

---

### Track 5 — I, You, He/She

**Teacher:** Now we have three fronts.

**Teacher:** Mo. I.

**Teacher:** O. You.

**Teacher:** Ó. He or she.

**Teacher:** Say them slowly.

**Student:** Mo. O. Ó.

**Teacher:** Good. Now build.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "You want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "She wants money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ owó.

---

### Track 6 — English Changes, Yoruba Holds

**Teacher:** In English we say:

**Teacher:** I want.

**Teacher:** You want.

**Teacher:** He wants.

**Teacher:** English changes want to wants.

**Teacher:** Yoruba does not do that here.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Ó fẹ́.

**Teacher:** The verb stayed still. The little word at the front did the work.

---

### Track 7 — Actions

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "You want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "She wants to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "He wants to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "You want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

---

### Track 8 — It Wants

**Teacher:** Ó can also mean it.

**Teacher:** We don't need much of that yet, but listen.

**Teacher:** "It wants water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Same Yoruba sentence as "he wants water" or "she wants water." Context tells us which thought we mean.

**Teacher:** We will not do more with "it" now. Just know that the word can carry that too.

---

### Track 9 — Final Round

**Teacher:** Final round. Listen for I, you, he, she.

**Teacher:** "You want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "She wants to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "He wants food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "You want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** O fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** "She wants money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Ó fẹ́ owó.

---

### Track 10 — Close

**Teacher:** Good. Today the tiny tone difference mattered.

**Teacher:** O fẹ́. You want.

**Teacher:** Ó fẹ́. He wants. She wants. It wants.

**Teacher:** Same mouth shape, different place in the voice, different word.

**Teacher:** And still, fẹ́ did not change.

**Teacher:** That is the win of this first little arc. You can change the person at the front and keep the engine steady.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ó` | he/she/it | medium | native performance must distinguish from `o` |

Structures introduced:

- pitch can distinguish `o` and `ó`,
- `ó` covers he/she/it,
- `fẹ́` remains unchanged across `mo`, `o`, and `ó`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- object `ó` / third-person copied vowel,
- `ó` dropping before `kò`,
- `wọn`,
- negation,
- questions,
- tense/aspect.
