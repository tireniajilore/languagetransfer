# Language Transfer Yoruba — Lesson 2, Draft v2

Status: draft. Not native-certified.

Audience: diaspora heritage learner. English-speaking. Has heard Yoruba at home, never studied it.

Method: Language Transfer / Thinking Method.

Medium: audio only. The learner never sees text.

Target length: 13-16 minutes at slow LT pace.

Main atom: same `Mo fẹ́ ...` frame, more control.

Teacher stance: the learner is learning that repetition can feel like new thought when the frame is reused with intention.

Likely learner errors:

- trying to recall whole phrases from Lesson 1,
- adding English "to" before action endings,
- expecting new grammar because a new lesson has begun,
- letting tone go flat when attention shifts to meaning.

---

## Notes for the Yoruba Speaker Rating This

This lesson deliberately adds very little grammar. It should feel like control, not expansion.

Please check:

1. Are `lọ`, `wá`, and `owó` appropriate beginner additions?
2. Are the tones marked well enough for a native speaker to perform?
3. Does `Mo fẹ́ wá` sound natural enough as a beginner drill sentence, or should it be replaced?
4. Is `Mo fẹ́ owó` acceptable as a simple drill sentence, or too blunt/odd?
5. Does the teacher voice feel patient and alive?

---

## Script

### Track 1 — Rebuild, Don't Remember

**Teacher:** Welcome back. We are going to do something very useful today: almost nothing new.

**Teacher:** That may sound disappointing, but it is actually where a language starts becoming yours.

**Teacher:** Don't try to remember the phrases from Lesson 1. Rebuild them.

**Teacher:** What was the little frame for "I want"?

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́. You found the frame.

**Teacher:** Now add water. "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi. Now sleep. "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ sùn. And notice, no panic if it took a moment. The moment you spent rebuilding is the useful part.

---

### Track 2 — The Frame Holds

**Teacher:** Say just the frame again.

**Student:** Mo fẹ́.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́. That is our little machine.

**Teacher:** Food was **oúnjẹ**. So "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Eat was **jẹun**. "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun. What changed between those two answers?

*[pause]*

**Student:** The ending.

**Teacher:** Exactly. The ending. The ending changed. The frame did not. This is the kind of boring observation that becomes very powerful if you keep making it.

---

### Track 3 — Go

**Teacher:** A new action now. The word for go is **lọ**. Lọ.

**Teacher:** Listen. Lọ. Low voice. Let it sit low.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Lọ. Build "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Possible learner mistake:** Mo fẹ́ to lọ.

**Teacher:** If English gave you a little "to" there, that is the old habit doing its job. We just don't hire it for Yoruba here. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Again. "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

---

### Track 4 — Come

**Teacher:** The word for come is **wá**. Wá.

**Teacher:** This one sits higher. Wá.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Wá.

**Teacher:** Good. Wá. Now build "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Yes. Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Let's keep the contrast alive.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ wá. Low ending, high ending. Same frame.

---

### Track 5 — Money

**Teacher:** One new thing. The word for money is **owó**. Owó.

**Teacher:** Listen first: owó.

**Teacher:** Try it.

**Student:** Owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Owó. Now, maybe this sentence is a little too honest, but it is structurally useful. "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** We will let a native speaker tell us if this is too blunt as a beginner sentence. For now, it helps us keep practicing a thing after `fẹ́`.

---

### Track 6 — Things and Actions

**Teacher:** Let's sort the endings without using grammar words.

**Teacher:** Omi. Water. A thing.

**Teacher:** Owó. Money. A thing.

**Teacher:** Lọ. Go. An action.

**Teacher:** Wá. Come. An action.

**Teacher:** Yoruba lets us keep the frame steady for both.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ wá. You are not learning a list. You are learning what the frame can tolerate.

---

### Track 7 — A Useful Trap

**Teacher:** Listen to the English for a moment.

**Teacher:** I want water.

**Teacher:** I want money.

**Teacher:** I want to sleep.

**Teacher:** I want to go.

**Teacher:** English changes its little furniture depending on what follows. Yoruba, here, stays calmer.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** This is why we are drilling one frame. We want your mind to trust it before we ask it to do anything heavier.

---

### Track 8 — Final Round

**Teacher:** Final round. Build each one from the frame.

**Teacher:** "I want to sleep."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ sùn.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ sùn. Low ending.

**Teacher:** "I want money."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ owó.

**Teacher:** "I want to come."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ wá. You kept `fẹ́` high even while thinking about the new word.

**Teacher:** "I want water."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ omi.

**Teacher:** "I want to go."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ lọ.

**Teacher:** "I want food."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ oúnjẹ.

**Teacher:** "I want to eat."

*[pause]*

**Student:** Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

**Teacher:** Good. Mo fẹ́ jẹun.

---

### Track 9 — Close

**Teacher:** Good. Today was not about collecting many words. It was about finding the same path again and again without it feeling like memorization.

**Teacher:** Mo fẹ́ omi. Mo fẹ́ lọ. Mo fẹ́ owó. Mo fẹ́ wá.

**Teacher:** Next lesson, we change the person at the front. The verb will stay still, which is a small mercy and we will absolutely take it.

## Vocabulary and Structure Introduced

| Yoruba | Meaning | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lọ` | go | medium | verify tone/performance |
| `wá` | come | medium | verify beginner naturalness in `Mo fẹ́ wá` |
| `owó` | money | medium | verify naturalness in `Mo fẹ́ owó` |

Structures introduced:

- no new grammar atom beyond stronger control of `Mo fẹ́ ...`,
- mixed noun endings and verb endings,
- continued delay of English infinitive `to`.

Structures intentionally delayed:

- `o`,
- `ó`,
- `sí`,
- object pronouns,
- negation,
- questions,
- future `máa`.
