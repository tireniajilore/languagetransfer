## Lesson 17: Yo Is Emphasis In Practice

Do not say yo every time English says I.

Quiero.

That already means I want.

[on screen: I want]
Quick check: say I want.
[beat]
Quiero.

The word yo means I.

But Spanish usually leaves it out, because the verb already tells you who.

Use yo when you want to stress who is doing it: I want it, not someone else.

Yo quiero.

That is not just neutral I want. It is more like me, I want.

[on screen: I want]
Say I want, with that me, not them feeling.
[beat]
Yo quiero.

For the negative, no still goes right before quiero.

[on screen: I do not want]
Say: I do not want, with emphasis.
[beat]
Yo no quiero.

Use yo when you are contrasting, correcting, or making your own choice clear.

Caption: Quiero already means I want. Use yo when you are contrasting, correcting, or making your own choice clear.

## Lesson 18: Me At The End

Spanish has a tiny trick for saying me.

In English, prepare me usually means prepare myself.

Start with prepare. In Spanish, preparar.

Preparar.

Now put me at the end.

[on screen: to prepare myself]
Say: to prepare myself.
[beat]
Prepararme.

Good. Same verb. You just attached me.

Now use the old ation pattern.

Organization gives organización, then organizar.

[on screen: to organize myself]
Now say: to organize myself.
[beat]
Organizarme.

One more. Information gives información, then informar.

[on screen: to inform me]
Attach me: to inform me.
[beat]
Informarme.

Same idea: inform me usually means inform myself.

And Spanish often uses that for find out.

[on screen: I want to find out]
Now say: I want to find out.
[long beat]
Quiero informarme.

Good. Do not learn a new sentence. Build the verb, then attach me.

Caption: Put me on the end of the verb: prepararme, organizarme, informarme. Quiero informarme = I want to find out.

## Lesson 19: Te At The End Means You

Spanish has the same tiny trick for saying you.

The word is te.

Here, te means you.

Start with inform. In Spanish, informar.

Informar.

Attach te at the end.

[on screen: to inform you]
Say: to inform you.
[beat]
Informarte.

Quiero.

Quick reminder: quiero means I want.

[on screen: I want to inform you]
Now say: I want to inform you.
[long beat]
Quiero informarte.

Good. Now we can handle a more serious verb.

Obligation becomes obligación, then obligar.

[on screen: to oblige you]
Attach you: to oblige you.
[beat]
Obligarte.


[on screen: I don't want to oblige you]
Say: I do not want to oblige you.
[long beat]
No quiero obligarte.

One stretch: provocation gives provocar.

[on screen: I don't want to provoke you]
Now say: I do not want to provoke you.
[long beat]
No quiero provocarte.

Good. Te stayed the same. Only the verb changed.

Caption: Te can mean you, and it can attach to the end of verbs: informarte, obligarte, provocarte.

## Lesson 20: Lo Means It Or Him

Spanish can turn cancel into cancel it without learning a new verb.

Lo.

Lo is the little piece that can mean it.

When the verb is unchanged, lo can attach to the end.

Cancel becomes cancelar.

Cancelar.

Add lo to the end, and you get to cancel it.

[on screen: to cancel it]
Test it first: to cancel it.
[beat]
Cancelarlo.

Quiero.

Quick reminder: quiero means I want.

[on screen: I want to cancel it]
Now you try the full sentence: I want to cancel it.
[long beat]
Quiero cancelarlo.

One more thing: lo can also mean him.

[on screen: to cancel him]
Same verb: to cancel him.
[beat]
Cancelarlo.

Salvation gives salvación, then salvar.

Salvar.

Add lo to the end, and you get to save him.

Salvarlo.

[on screen: I want to save him]
Now say: I want to save him.
[long beat]
Quiero salvarlo.

Good. You learned where lo goes before you had to build the sentence.

Caption: Lo can mean it or him, and it can attach to the end of an unchanged verb: cancelarlo, salvarlo.

## Lesson 21: The Spanish R Is Not A Crisis

The Spanish are sound scares people more than it should.

At the start of a word, this sound gets the stronger flick.

[on screen: regular]
Try the English word regular, with Spanish sound.
[beat]
En español, regular.

[on screen: real]
And real, with that same first sound.
[beat]
En español, real.

Inside a word, a single are sound is lighter.

Pero.

But double are gets the stronger sound again.

Perro.

If the strong sound does not come out yet, do not let it stop you.

Keep listening. Keep the mouth relaxed. The sound can arrive later.

Caption: Initial r and double rr use the stronger Spanish R: regular, real, perro. A single r inside a word is lighter: pero.

## Lesson 22: Take The Long Route To Verbs

The slow route is how you stop guessing Spanish verbs.

Do not jump from the short English verb.

Use the bigger English noun first.

Generate can go through generation.

Generación.

Then take off ción and add ar.

[on screen: to generate]
So how would you say to generate?
[beat]
Generar.

Same long route: situation becomes situación.

[on screen: to situate]
Now turn it into: to situate.
[beat]
Situar.

Participation becomes participación.

[on screen: to participate]
Take it slowly: to participate.
[beat]
Participar.

Creation gives the same pattern.

[on screen: to create]
Now say: to create.
[beat]
Crear.

Good. The long route is slower for one second, and safer forever.

Caption: Don't jump from the short English verb. Use the long route: generation -> generación -> generar, participation -> participación -> participar.

## Lesson 23: ENCE And ANCE Become Spanish Too

English has another Spanish word machine hiding in plain sight.

When English ends in the ending spelled E N C E, Spanish often has encia.

Experience becomes experiencia.

Experiencia.

[on screen: difference]
You know different. Now try difference.
[beat]
Diferencia.

[on screen: influence]
What about influence?
[beat]
Influencia.

And when English ends in the ending spelled A N C E, Spanish often has ancia.

Important gave you importante. Now importance gives you importancia.

Importancia.

[on screen: distance]
Try distance.
[beat]
Distancia.

[on screen: consistency]
One more: consistency.
[beat]
Consistencia.

Good. You are not memorizing nouns. You are collecting word machines.

Caption: English -ence and -ance often become Spanish -encia and -ancia: diferencia, influencia, importancia, distancia.
