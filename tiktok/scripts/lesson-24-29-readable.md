## Lesson 24: Intento With Simple Verbs

One Spanish word lets you say I try and I am trying.

Intento.

Intento can mean I try, or I am trying. Context does the rest.

So keep the verb easy first. Prepare is preparar.

Preparar.

[on screen: I am trying to prepare]
Say: I am trying to prepare.
[long beat]
Intento preparar.

Cancel is cancelar. Same sentence shape.

[on screen: I am trying to cancel]
Say: I am trying to cancel.
[long beat]
Intento cancelar.

Good. Now one tiny stretch. Lo can mean it, and it attaches at the end.

[on screen: I am trying to cancel it]
Say: I am trying to cancel it.
[long beat]
Intento cancelarlo.

That is the point. Intento stays still while the verb changes.

Caption: Intento means I try or I am trying. Keep it simple first: intento preparar, intento cancelar, then stretch to intento cancelarlo.

## Lesson 25: Spanish H Disappears

Spanish has one letter you should completely ignore.

The letter h is written, but it does not make a sound.

C H together still makes the ch sound. But a plain h disappears.

Do not even give it a tiny breath. Imagine the word starts with the next vowel.

[on screen: hospital]
Try the word hospital, with no h sound.
[beat]
Hospital.

[on screen: horrible]
Now horrible. No h sound.
[beat]
Horrible.

[on screen: habitual]
What about habitual?
[beat]
Habitual.

And if you add mente, the h still stays silent.

[on screen: habitually]
Say habitually.
[beat]
Habitualmente.

The word for now also hides one of these silent h's.

Ahora.

Good. The letter is there for your eyes, not your mouth.

Caption: Spanish h is silent: hospital, horrible, habitual, habitualmente, ahora. See it, but do not pronounce it.

## Lesson 26: Build A Long Sentence With Ahora

Let's build a long Spanish sentence one block at a time.

Ahora means now.

Ahora.

[on screen: now]
Quick check: say now.
[beat]
Ahora.

Pero means but.

Pero.

[on screen: but]
Quick check: say but.
[beat]
Pero.

And cancel it is cancelarlo. The lo goes at the end.

[on screen: to cancel it]
Say: to cancel it.
[beat]
Cancelarlo.

[on screen: I want to cancel it]
Now add quiero: I want to cancel it.
[long beat]
Quiero cancelarlo.

[on screen: I want to cancel it now]
Add ahora: I want to cancel it now.
[long beat]
Quiero cancelarlo ahora.

For the negative, Spanish parks no in front.

[on screen: I don't want to cancel it]
Take one step back: I do not want to cancel it.
[long beat]
No quiero cancelarlo.

[on screen: I don't want to cancel it now]
Now add now: I do not want to cancel it now.
[long beat]
No quiero cancelarlo ahora.

Now we have both halves. First: I want to cancel it. Then: but. Then: I do not want to cancel it now.

[on screen: I want to cancel it, but I don't want to cancel it now]
Build the whole sentence.
[long beat]
Quiero cancelarlo, pero no quiero cancelarlo ahora.

Good. You did not jump to the long sentence. You built it.

Caption: Build a long Spanish sentence from small pieces: quiero cancelarlo, pero no quiero cancelarlo ahora.

## Lesson 27: Voy Means I Am Going

Spanish gives you one tiny word for I go and I am going.

Voy.

Voy can mean I go, or I am going. Context does the work.

And ahora means now.

[on screen: I am going now]
Say: I am going now.
[beat]
Voy ahora.

To make it negative, park no in front.

[on screen: I am not going now]
Now say: I am not going now.
[long beat]
No voy ahora.

One more. Pero means but.

Pero.

[on screen: I am going, but I am not going now]
Say: I am going, but I am not going now.
[long beat]
Voy, pero no voy ahora.

Good. Voy stayed still. No and ahora did the moving.

Caption: Voy means I go or I am going. Add ahora, park no in front, then build: voy, pero no voy ahora.

## Lesson 28: Visit Him With One Ending

English sometimes hides the Spanish verb in the bigger word.

Think of visitation. That points you to visitar.

Visitar.

That means to visit.

Lo can mean him, and it attaches to the end.

[on screen: to visit him]
Say: to visit him.
[beat]
Visitarlo.

Quiero means I want.

[on screen: I want to visit him]
Now say: I want to visit him.
[long beat]
Quiero visitarlo.

Good. You found the verb through a bigger English word, then attached lo.

Caption: Use the bigger English word to find the Spanish verb: visitation -> visitar. Then attach lo: quiero visitarlo.

## Lesson 29: Spanish V Is Often A Soft B

Spanish v often does not sound like English v.

In much of the Spanish-speaking world, it sounds closer to a soft b.

Not a hard English b with a burst of air. Softer.

You already saw it in voy.

Voy.

[on screen: visible]
Try visible with that soft Spanish sound.
[beat]
Visible.

[on screen: viral]
Now viral.
[beat]
Viral.

[on screen: virus]
And virus.
[beat]
Virus.

One more pattern word. Convenient becomes conveniente.

[on screen: convenient]
Say convenient in Spanish.
[beat]
Conveniente.

Good. Same letters, but your mouth changed languages.

Caption: Spanish v often sounds close to a soft b: voy, visible, viral, virus, conveniente.
