## Lesson 30: Find Continue From Continuation

When you forget a Spanish word, do not ask, do I remember it?

Ask, what English cousin can help me find it?

If carry on feels hard, switch the English first.

Instead of I want to carry on, you can say I want to continue it.

Continue gives you continuation.

Use the pattern you already know: ation becomes ación.

Continuación.

Then take off that noun ending and add ar.

Continuar.

Quiero means I want.

[on screen: I want to continue]
Say: I want to continue.
[long beat]
Quiero continuar.

Lo can mean it, and it attaches to the end.

[on screen: I want to continue it]
Now say: I want to continue it.
[long beat]
Quiero continuarlo.

One more. Intento means I try, or I am trying.

[on screen: I am trying to continue]
Say: I am trying to continue.
[long beat]
Intento continuar.

Good. You did not remember a word. You found it.

Caption: If carry on feels hard, switch the English first: continue -> continuation -> continuación -> continuar -> continuarlo.

## Lesson 31: Demonstration Hides Show

Spanish can hide an everyday verb inside a fancy English word.

The everyday verb is show. The fancy English clue is demonstration.

Use the old pattern first: ation becomes ación.

Demostración.

Notice that English n did not come with us.

Now take off that noun ending and add ar.

[on screen: to demonstrate]
Say: to demonstrate.
[beat]
Demostrar.

Now remove the de at the front.

[on screen: demostrar - de]
What do you get?
[beat]
Mostrar.

Mostrar means to show.

[on screen: to show]
Quick check: say to show.
[beat]
Mostrar.

Good. Demonstration gave you demonstrate, and demonstrate gave you show.

Caption: Demonstration can lead you to demostrar. Remove de, and you get mostrar: to show.

## Lesson 32: Show You Something

You can build I want to show you something from four pieces.

First we need to show. The clue is demonstration.

Demonstration gives you the Spanish noun first.

Demostración.

Then take off that noun ending and add ar.

Demostrar.

Take away the de, and you get mostrar.

Mostrar.

Mostrar means to show.

Now te means you, and it can attach to the end.

[on screen: to show you]
Say: to show you.
[beat]
Mostrarte.

Algo means something.

Algo.

[on screen: to show you something]
Now say: to show you something.
[long beat]
Mostrarte algo.

Quiero means I want.

Quiero.

[on screen: I want to show you something]
Add quiero: I want to show you something.
[long beat]
Quiero mostrarte algo.

Good. You did not translate word by word. You stacked the pieces.

Caption: Get mostrar from demonstration -> demostración -> demostrar -> mostrar. Then build: quiero mostrarte algo.

## Lesson 33: Voy A Builds The Future

Spanish gives you a future sentence without a new verb ending.

Voy can mean I go, or I am going.

Voy.

To say I am going to, Spanish uses voy a.

Voy a.

After voy a, keep the next verb unchanged.

Continue is continuar.

[on screen: I am going to continue]
Say: I am going to continue.
[long beat]
Voy a continuar.

Confiscation can give you confiscar. Add lo for it.

[on screen: I am going to confiscate it]
Now say: I am going to confiscate it.
[long beat]
Voy a confiscarlo.

Examination can give you examinar. Same shape.

[on screen: I am going to examine it]
Say: I am going to examine it.
[long beat]
Voy a examinarlo.

Good. Voy a did the future work. The verb stayed in its to form.

Caption: Voy a means I am going to. Then keep the next verb unchanged: voy a continuar, voy a confiscarlo, voy a examinarlo.

## Lesson 34: Lo Becomes Los

One tiny sound turns it or him into them.

Lo can mean it or him.

Lo.

Make it plural, and you get los: them.

Los.

To see is ver. Think of view.

Ver.

Attach los to the end.

[on screen: to see them]
Say: to see them.
[beat]
Verlos.

Quiero means I want.

[on screen: I want to see them]
Say: I want to see them.
[long beat]
Quiero verlos.

Intento means I am trying.

[on screen: I am trying to see them]
Say: I am trying to see them.
[long beat]
Intento verlos.

Good. Lo became los, and verlos moved as one piece.

Caption: Lo can mean it or him. Add s for los, them. Then attach it: verlos, quiero verlos, intento verlos.

## Lesson 35: Movement Verbs Take A

This little Spanish a is not random.

It shows up after a movement verb, when another action comes next.

Voy means I am going. Going is movement.

So before the next action, Spanish says voy a.

See them is verlos.

[on screen: I am going to see them]
Say: I am going to see them.
[long beat]
Voy a verlos.

Now add try. Intentar means to try, but trying is not movement.

So the a belongs after voy. Then go straight into intentar verlos.

[on screen: I am going to try to see them]
Now say: I am going to try to see them.
[long beat]
Voy a intentar verlos.

Another movement verb is pasar: to pass, or to pass by.

Pasar.

Because pasar has movement, it also uses the bridge before the next action.

Visit is visitar. Te means you, so visitarte means to visit you.

[on screen: to pass by to visit you]
Say: to pass by to visit you.
[long beat]
Pasar a visitarte.

Now combine voy and pasar. Both have movement, so both get the bridge.

[on screen: I am going to pass by to visit you]
Now say: I am going to pass by to visit you.
[long beat]
Voy a pasar a visitarte.

One stretch. Show you something is mostrarte algo.

[on screen: I want to pass by to show you something]
Say: I want to pass by to show you something.
[long beat]
Quiero pasar a mostrarte algo.

Good. Listen for movement first. That tells you where the a goes.

Caption: The little a comes after movement verbs when another action follows: voy a verlos, pasar a visitarte, voy a pasar a visitarte.
