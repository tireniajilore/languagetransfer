# TikTok Lesson Pack: Lessons 2–4 (Standalone)

Source topic: Language Transfer Spanish Lesson 2 — Spanish vowels, `es`, and
`no es`. Numbered as sequential standalone micro-lessons (Lesson 1 = the `-al`
cognates clip). Each stands alone in the feed — no "part two."

**Design rule (the LT order): teach the building block first, THEN prompt the
viewer to use it with examples.** Never quiz a piece the script hasn't taught
yet — the viewer is always equipped to answer, so the pause is a win, not a
trap. The hook is a promise/claim (scroll-stop), not a cold quiz.

Stage directions in `[brackets]` are for the editor, not spoken:
- `[on screen: ...]` = burn-in text shown while the line is delivered.
- `[beat]` = hold a silent pause (~1.5s) so the viewer answers before the reveal.

---

## Lesson 2: Spanish Vowels Make The Accent

Concept: Teach the five fixed Spanish vowels first, then let the viewer read the
words. The final stress is shown through the examples, not stated as a rule (so
no risk of overgeneralizing — most Spanish words don't stress the last syllable).

Spoken transcript:

```text
Reading Spanish out loud is way easier than English. Here's the reason.

English vowels are chaos.
A says "Apple". "May." "About". One letter, three sounds.

Spanish vowels never move. Five sounds, locked:
A is ah. E is eh. I is ee. O is oh. U is oo.
That's the whole system.

So now you can read these.

[on screen: natural]   Your turn.   [beat]   na-too-RAL.   Good.
[on screen: metal]     [beat]   meh-TAL.   Good.
[on screen: legal]     [beat]   leh-GAL.   Good.
[on screen: ideal]     [beat]   ee-deh-AL.   Good.

You didn't memorize four words.
You learned five sounds and now you can read all of them.
```

Caption:

```text
Spanish is easier to read out loud than English — the vowels never change: a-e-i-o-u = ah-eh-ee-oh-oo. Then read these: natural, metal, legal, ideal. #learnspanish #spanishtok #spanish
```

## Lesson 3: One Tiny Word Builds Sentences

Concept: Teach `es` ("is") and the clean S first, then prompt the viewer to build
sentences with cognates they already know. The he/she/it collapse is revealed
through use, after `es` is taught.

Spoken transcript:

```text
One tiny Spanish word builds a whole pile of sentences. Here it is.

[on screen: es]
Es. It means "is."
And keep that S clean — not "ez." Es.

You already know words like normal, legal, natural.
So now put it together.

[on screen: it is normal]    It is normal?    [beat]   Es normal.   Good.
[on screen: it is legal]     It is legal?     [beat]   Es legal.   Good.
[on screen: it is natural]   It is natural?   [beat]   Es natural.   Good.

Es is also the same word for he and she.
For example, try: he is liberal?   [beat]   Es liberal.   Good.
She is ideal?   [beat]   Es ideal.

He, she, it — Spanish doesn't care. We just use "es."
```

Caption:

```text
First learn "es" = is. Then you can build: es normal, es legal, es natural, es ideal — and it never changes for he/she/it. Keep the S clean (not "ez"). #learnspanish #spanishtok #spanish
```

## Lesson 4: Make It Negative With No Es

Concept: Re-establish `es`, teach the one move (put `no` in front), then prompt
the negatives. The payoff is how little the viewer had to do.

Spoken transcript:

```text
If you can say it in Spanish, you can already say the opposite.

[on screen: es normal]
"Es normal" means "it's normal."
To make it negative, Spanish does one thing: it puts "no" in front.

No es normal. That's "it's not normal."

Now you try: (say it)   [beat]   No es normal.   Good.
[on screen: it's not legal]     What about, it's not legal?   [beat]   No es legal.   Good.
[on screen: it's not ideal]     It's not ideal?   [beat]   No es ideal.
[on screen: it's not natural]   It's not natural?   [beat]   No es natural.

Feel the flip:
Es normal. No es normal.
Es legal. No es legal.

You just park "no" in front. That's the whole move.
```

Caption:

```text
The easiest Spanish negative: learn that "no" goes in front of "es." Then: no es normal, no es legal, no es ideal. Nothing else changes. #learnspanish #spanishtok #spanish
```
