## Lesson 13: Quiero And No Quiero

You may already know how to say I love you in Spanish.

Hidden inside it is one of the most useful Spanish phrases.

Te quiero.

Literally, that is I want you.

Quiero.

That means I want.

[on screen: I want]
Your turn: I want.
[beat]
Quiero.

To make it negative, Spanish does one very kind thing.

Park no in front.

[on screen: I don't want]
Now say: I do not want.
[beat]
No quiero.

Now make it a full sentence. Coffee is café, with Spanish sound.

Café.

[on screen: I want coffee]
Say: I want coffee.
[beat]
Quiero café.

[on screen: I don't want coffee]
And now: I do not want coffee.
[beat]
No quiero café.

Good. Same sentence, one word parked in front.

Caption: Quiero means I want. To say I don't want coffee, Spanish just parks no in front: quiero café -> no quiero café.

## Lesson 14: TION Words Become CION

That common English ending is secretly a Spanish word machine.

When English says tion, Spanish often writes cion.

But Spanish puts the stress at the end.

[on screen: preparation]
Try the English word preparation.
[beat]
En español, preparación.

[on screen: organization]
What about organization?
[beat]
En español, organización.

[on screen: generation]
Now generation.
[beat]
En español, generación.

Hear that first G? In Spanish, it has the soft throat sound.

[on screen: generation]
Try it again: generation.
[beat]
En español, generación.

[on screen: confirmation]
And confirmation.
[beat]
En español, confirmación.

Good. You used English as a map, then cleaned off the English sound.

Caption: English -tion words often become Spanish -ción words: preparación, organización, generación, confirmación. Stress the end.

## Lesson 15: Turn ACION Words Into Verbs

One English ation word can give you a Spanish noun and a Spanish verb.

First, the English ending ation.

En español, ación.

Then take that ending off, and add ar.

Preparación.

That is preparation.

Preparar.

That is to prepare.

[on screen: generation]
Try this one: generation.
[beat]
Generación.

[on screen: to generate]
Now turn it into: to generate.
[beat]
Generar.

[on screen: confirmation]
What about confirmation?
[beat]
Confirmación.

[on screen: to confirm]
Now: to confirm.
[beat]
Confirmar.

[on screen: complication]
One more: complication.
[beat]
Complicación.

[on screen: to complicate]
Now: to complicate.
[beat]
Complicar.

Good. You did not learn four verbs. You unlocked a pattern.

Caption: One English -ation word can give you two Spanish words: preparación -> preparar, generación -> generar.

## Lesson 16: Build Sentences With Quiero

You do not need a big vocabulary to say real Spanish sentences.

You just need one phrase and a verb pattern.

Quiero.

That means I want.

[on screen: I want]
Quick check: say I want.
[beat]
Quiero.

Good. Now we need the second piece: to prepare, which we can build from English.

From prepare, think preparation.

Preparation becomes preparación.

Preparación.

Then take off ción and add ar.

Preparar.

[on screen: I want to prepare]
Say: I want to prepare.
[long beat]
Quiero preparar.

Good. Now apply that same build to other examples.

[on screen: I want to cooperate]
Now: I want to cooperate.
[long beat]
Quiero cooperar.

[on screen: I want to cancel]
Say: I want to cancel.
[long beat]
Quiero cancelar.

And the negative is still just no in front.

[on screen: I don't want to cancel]
Say: I do not want to cancel.
[long beat]
No quiero cancelar.

[on screen: I want to explore]
Last one: I want to explore.
[long beat]
Quiero explorar.

Good. You stacked one known piece onto another.

Caption: Quiero + an unchanged -ar verb gives you real sentences: quiero preparar, quiero cooperar, quiero cancelar, no quiero cancelar.
