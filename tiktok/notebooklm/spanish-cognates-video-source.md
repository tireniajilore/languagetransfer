# You Already Speak Spanish: The Cognate System

A source document for a NotebookLM Video Overview. Audience: absolute-beginner
English speakers who think Spanish is hard. Goal: prove they already recognize
~1,000 Spanish words, then hand them five patterns that convert English words
into Spanish on the spot. Warm, encouraging, concrete. No grammar jargon.

---

## The big idea

English and Spanish share thousands of "cognates" — words that are nearly
identical in both languages because they came from the same Latin roots. If you
speak English, you have already met most of these words. You do not need to
memorize them. You need a few small rules that tell you how the English version
turns into the Spanish version.

The single most important rule is where you put the stress. English tends to
stress the front of a word. Spanish very often stresses the end. Move the
emphasis to the end and slow down, and an English word can come out sounding
Spanish.

Say these out loud, pushing the stress to the last syllable: animal, hospital,
capital, natural. Those are real Spanish words, and you just said them.

---

## Pattern 1 — Vowels never drift

In Spanish the five vowels each make one steady sound, every time:
a = "ah", e = "eh", i = "ee", o = "oh", u = "oo". English vowels wander;
Spanish vowels do not. That is why Spanish is easier to read out loud than
English. Try reading these with pure vowels: natural, metal, legal, ideal.

## Pattern 2 — "-tion" becomes "-ción"

English words ending in -tion almost always have a Spanish twin ending in
-ción, with the stress on that final syllable. Preparation becomes preparación.
Organization becomes organización. Generation becomes generación. One ending
swap unlocks hundreds of words.

## Pattern 3 — "-ent" and "-ant" become "-ente" and "-ante"

Add a vowel to the end and you often land on the Spanish word. Important
becomes importante. Different becomes diferente. Constant becomes constante.
Excellent becomes excelente.

## Pattern 4 — "-ly" becomes "-mente"

Where English adds -ly to make an adverb, Spanish adds -mente. Normally is
normalmente. Really is realmente. Naturally is naturalmente. Same move every
time: take the word, attach -mente.

## Pattern 5 — "-able" and "-ible" carry straight over

These words barely change; your mouth just switches languages. Possible is
posible. Probable is probable. Flexible is flexible. Say them with Spanish
vowels and the stress at the end.

---

## Stacking pieces into sentences

Once you have a pile of words, you need almost nothing to make sentences. The
tiny word "es" means "it is." So: es normal, es legal, es importante. Put "no"
in front to make it negative: no es normal, no es importante. Add an -mente
adverb on the front and you have a full, natural sentence: normalmente es
importante — "normally, it is important." You are not memorizing phrases. You
are building them from parts you already own.

---

## The mindset that makes it work

Do not hunt for brand-new words. Listen for the English word hiding inside the
Spanish one, then clean off the English sound: pure vowels, stress at the end,
relaxed mouth. You are not starting from zero. You are translating a vocabulary
you already have.

---

## Suggested closing line

You already speak more Spanish than you think. You just needed someone to show
you the patterns — and now you have five of them.
